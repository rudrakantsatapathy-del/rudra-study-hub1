🧠 Quick revision

Magnetic field: the region around a magnet or current-carrying conductor where magnetic force can be detected.

Field-line rules: outside a magnet, lines go from north to south; field lines never cross; closer lines mean a stronger field.

Right-hand thumb rule: point your right thumb in the direction of conventional current; curled fingers show the magnetic-field direction.

Chapter notes

Class 10 level summaries

Concept 01

Magnetic field and field lines

Magnetic field lines represent the direction and relative strength of a magnetic field.

  • The tangent at a point gives the field direction.
  • Lines form continuous closed curves.
  • They never intersect one another.
  • They are closer together where the field is stronger.
Concept 02

Straight current-carrying conductor

The magnetic field lines are concentric circles around a straight conductor. Their direction depends on current direction.

Right-hand thumb rule: thumb = conventional current; curled fingers = magnetic field.

Concept 03

Circular current loop

Each part of the loop produces a magnetic field. Near the centre, the contributions reinforce one another and the field lines are more nearly straight.

  • Increasing current increases the field.
  • Increasing the number of turns increases the field.
  • Field direction depends on current direction.
Concept 04

Solenoid and electromagnet

A solenoid is a coil of many circular turns. When current flows, it produces a magnetic field similar to a bar magnet.

  • The field inside a long solenoid is nearly uniform.
  • A soft-iron core can make the electromagnet stronger.
  • Electromagnets can be switched on and off by controlling current.
Concept 05

Force and electric motor

A current-carrying conductor placed in a magnetic field can experience a force. The direction depends on current and magnetic field directions.

Fleming's left-hand rule: forefinger shows field, middle finger shows conventional current, and thumb shows force/motion. Keep the three mutually perpendicular.

An electric motor converts electrical energy into mechanical energy.

Concept 06

Electromagnetic induction

A changing magnetic field through a coil can induce current in the coil.

  • Relative motion between a magnet and coil can change magnetic flux.
  • The induced current direction depends on how the magnetic field changes.
  • An electric generator uses electromagnetic induction to convert mechanical energy into electrical energy.

Important board-style questions

Write answers in your notebook before revealing hints

✍️ Short answers

1. State the right-hand thumb rule.

Imagine holding a straight conductor in your right hand with the thumb pointing in the direction of conventional current. The curled fingers indicate the direction of magnetic field lines.

2. Why do magnetic field lines never intersect?

If they intersected, the field would have two directions at the same point, which is impossible.

3. What is a solenoid?

A long cylindrical coil made of many closely wound turns of insulated conducting wire.

📝 Explain / draw

4. Draw the field around a straight wire.

Draw the conductor and concentric circular field lines around it. Add arrows to show the field direction and label the current direction.

5. Explain the working principle of an electric motor.

A current-carrying coil in a magnetic field experiences forces on opposite sides in opposite directions, producing a turning effect. A split-ring commutator reverses coil current every half turn to maintain rotation.

6. What is electromagnetic induction?

It is the production of induced current or emf when the magnetic field linked with a conductor or coil changes.

🔎 Check your diagrams

  • Use a sharp pencil and ruler where needed.
  • Label the current and field directions.
  • Use arrowheads on field lines.
  • Keep the diagram large and uncluttered.
  • Practise the motor diagram if it is included in your class syllabus.
